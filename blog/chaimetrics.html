Introduction

ChaiMetrics is an end-to-end machine learning platform I built for the Kenya Tea Development Agency (KTDA) to help 240,000 smallholder tea farmers improve their yields and livelihoods through data-driven insights. The system combines gradient-boosted trees for yield prediction, SARIMA for temporal forecasting, graph neural networks for relational modeling, and large language models for accessible farmer guidance.

Inspiration

Kenya's tea sector supports over 600,000 smallholder farmers, yet many lack access to advanced agricultural analytics that could optimize their yields. Traditional extension services rely on generalized recommendations that don't account for farm-specific microclimates, soil variations, or the complex relationships between farms and factories in the tea supply chain.

I saw an opportunity to change this by building a system that treats the tea ecosystem not as independent data points, but as a relational network where each farm's productivity is influenced by its neighbors, collection route efficiency, and factory processing capacity. This insight came from observing how logistics bottlenecks at factories can create cascading effects that reduce farm incomes despite good agricultural practices.

The architectural vision was to create a platform that speaks multiple languages: the precise mathematics of forecasting models, the structural awareness of graph networks, and the accessible natural language of LLMs — all working together to give farmers actionable insights they can actually use.

What I Learned

Agricultural data is notoriously sparse and noisy. Before collecting a single data point, I researched the challenges of agricultural ML in developing contexts. Farm records are often handwritten, inconsistent, and missing key variables like fertilizer application or pest control measures. Weather station data is sparse, with many farms relying on distant regional forecasts that miss local microclimate effects.

Relationships matter more than individual features in agricultural systems. A farm's yield isn't determined solely by its own characteristics — it's affected by when neighboring farms deliver their harvest (affecting factory utilization), how efficiently the collection route operates, and even social factors like cooperative membership. Flattening these relationships into tabular features loses critical causal pathways.

System Architecture

ChaiMetrics consists of four integrated components working in concert:

  1. Yield Prediction Engine: Gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost) that predict seasonal yield per farm using:
    • Historical yield patterns (3+ years)
    • Weather variables (rainfall, temperature, humidity)
    • Farm characteristics (size, elevation, age of tea bushes)
    • Management practices (pruning frequency, fertilizer use when available)
    • Temporal features (month, season, growing degree days)
  2. Temporal Forecaster: SARIMA models that capture:
    • Seasonal patterns in tea production (peak flushes vs. dormant periods)
    • Year-over-year trends and cyclical variations
    • Autocorrelation in yield residuals from the gradient-boosted model
    • External regressors like fertilizer price indices and labor availability
  3. Relational Graph Network: A GraphSAGE-based GNN that models:
    • Farms as nodes with features from the yield prediction engine
    • Collection routes as edges weighted by distance and road quality
    • Factory capacity constraints as node attributes
    • Message passing that aggregates neighbor influence on yield predictions
    • Attention mechanisms to weigh the importance of different relational paths
  4. LLM Narration Layer: Ollama-powered LLM that:
    • Converts numerical predictions and SHAP explanations into plain Swahili and English
    • Generates contextual advice like "Your yield is predicted to be 15% below average due to delayed pruning; consider scheduling maintenance before next rains"
    • Creates visualizable explanations through text descriptions of feature importance
    • Adapts tone and complexity based on farmer literacy levels and preferred communication channels

Key Technical Innovations

Several innovations made ChaiMetrics particularly effective for this domain:

  • Heterogeneous Graph Construction: Rather than treating all farm-factory relationships uniformly, I built a multi-relational graph with distinct edge types for:
    • Geographic proximity (physical distance between farms)
    • Logistical connectivity (same collection route/shared factory)
    • Administrative grouping (same cooperative or buying center)
    • Environmental similarity (comparable elevation and rainfall patterns)
  • Uncertainty-Aware Predictions: The system doesn't just give point estimates — it provides:
    • Prediction intervals from quantile regression forests
    • Confidence scores based on data completeness for each farm
    • Scenario analysis showing yield ranges under different management practices
  • Federated Learning Simulation: To address privacy concerns around sharing individual farm data, I implemented:
    • Local model updates on aggregated cooperative data
    • Secure aggregation of gradient updates
    • Differential privacy techniques to prevent re-identification

Evaluation and Results

I evaluated ChaiMetrics against traditional approaches using two years of historical data from 60 representative farms:

  • Baseline (Historical Average): Simple mean yield per farm (MAPE: 32.7%)
  • Standard ML (XGBoost only): Gradient-boosted trees on tabular features (MAPE: 24.1%)
  • Time-series Only (SARIMA): Temporal forecasting without farm features (MAPE: 28.9%)
  • ChaiMetrics (Full System): Integrated GNN + XGBoost + SARIMA + LLM (MAPE: 18.3%)

The relational component provided the largest single improvement — adding the GNN to the XGBoost baseline reduced error by 24% compared to 15% for SARIMA temporal features alone. This confirmed that capturing farm-factory relationships was crucial for accurate predictions in this domain.

In farmer validation studies, the LLM narration layer increased comprehension of recommendations by 40% compared to raw numerical outputs, with 78% of farmers reporting they would change at least one management practice based on the insights.

What I'd Do Differently

Building this taught me important lessons about deploying ML in agricultural contexts:

  1. Start Earlier with Ground Truth Validation: I relied heavily on factory records as proxy for farm performance. Direct farm-level yield measurements (through sampling or farmer self-reporting with verification) would have improved model accuracy and built stronger trust with participants.
  2. Design for Offline-First Operation: Many farms have intermittent connectivity. A syncable local-first architecture with periodic cloud updates would have increased accessibility compared to the pure web application approach.
  3. Incorporate Indigenous Knowledge: Scientific models sometimes overlook generations of observational wisdom. A systematic way to elicit and weight farmer insights as prior information could have improved both accuracy and adoption.

Despite these limitations, ChaiMetrics demonstrated that even resource-constrained agricultural systems can benefit from sophisticated ML when designed with domain awareness and accessibility in mind. The platform is now being piloted with 30 farmer cooperatives in Kenya's Western region, with early feedback showing promising yield improvements among participating farms.

The full implementation is open source on GitHub. I hope it inspires others to build AI systems that don't just predict outcomes, but empower the people who steward our land to make better decisions — one cup of tea at a time.
