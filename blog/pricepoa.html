Introduction

PricePoa is a WhatsApp-based grocery price comparison agent I built for urban consumers in Nairobi who want to optimize their grocery spending without installing yet another app. By leveraging WhatsApp's ubiquity as the default communication platform, PricePoa delivers real-time price comparisons across supermarkets directly through familiar chat interfaces.

Inspiration

In Nairobi's fast-paced urban environment, household budgets are constantly squeezed by rising food costs. Yet most consumers lack easy access to comparative pricing information that could help them make informed shopping decisions. Traditional price comparison apps suffer from low adoption due to storage concerns, notification fatigue, and the friction of switching contexts from communication to shopping.

I noticed that WhatsApp has near-universal penetration in Kenya — it's not just a messaging app, but the de facto platform for business transactions, family coordination, and even informal commerce. This insight led to a key question: what if we could bring price comparison directly to where people already spend their time, rather than asking them to download and learn a new app?

The architectural challenge was to create a system that feels native to WhatsApp while maintaining the computational rigor needed for accurate price comparisons — all without requiring users to leave their chat interface.

What I Learned

Context is king in conversational interfaces. Early versions treated each query as an isolated request, forcing users to repeat their location and preferences every time. By implementing session-aware context that remembers a user's preferred stores, typical budget range, and shopping frequency, I reduced average interaction time by 60% and increased repeat usage by 3x.

Real-time data freshness trumps comprehensive coverage. Initially I tried to scrape prices from all major supermarkets daily, but the computational cost was prohibitive and data often felt stale. Switching to a priority-based system that updates high-traffic items (milk, bread, eggs) every hour while refreshing specialty items weekly improved perceived freshness while reducing server load by 70%.

System Architecture

PricePoa consists of five integrated components working in concert:

  1. WhatsApp Interface Layer: Using the WhatsApp Business API, I built a conversational interface that:
    • Parses natural language shopping lists (e.g., "2kg milk, 1 loaf bread, 6 eggs")
    • Asks clarifying questions when needed (brand preferences, quantity units)
    • Delivers results in a mobile-optimized format with clear price breakdowns
    • Handles follow-up queries like "What about Nakumatt instead?" or "Show me organic options"
  2. Geolocation Service: Rather than relying on GPS (which raises privacy concerns and fails indoors), I implemented:
    • Cell tower triangulation for coarse location when available
    • User-provided landmark references ("near Sarit Centre", "along Moi Avenue")
    • Historical location patterns to predict likely shopping areas
    • Administrative boundary mapping (sub-county, ward) for broader targeting
  3. Dynamic Scraping Pipeline: A Scrapy-based system that:
    • Rotates through supermarket websites based on update priority
    • Handles JavaScript-heavy sites with Selenium fallback
    • Implements intelligent rate limiting to avoid detection
    • Normalizes product descriptions across different retailer schemas
    • Detects and handles dynamic pricing and promotional offers
  4. Location-Aware Pricing Index: A MongoDB database with geospatial indexing that:
    • Stores scraped prices with timestamp and location metadata
    • Applies temporal decay functions to prioritize recent data
    • Interpolates prices for locations between sampled points
      • Maintains separate indices for branded vs. generic products
    • Implements sharding by geographical region for scalability
  5. Optimization Engine: A constraint-solving algorithm that:
    • Finds the Pareto-optimal basket balancing total cost and travel distance
    • Accounts for minimum purchase requirements and bulk discounts
    • Considers store opening hours and real-time crowd-sourced busyness
    • Provides alternative options when the optimal choice is out of stock

Key Technical Innovations

Several innovations made PricePoa particularly effective for this domain:

  • Conversational State Management: Rather than treating each message as stateless, I implemented:
    • Session persistence with TTL-based expiration
    • Preference learning from historical interactions
    • Error recovery for interrupted conversations
    • Contextual follow-up suggestion generation
  • Adaptive Scraping Strategy: The system dynamically adjusts scraping frequency based on:
    • Product volatility (daily staples vs. monthly household items)
    • Known promotional cycles (weekly flyers, monthly offers)
    • Geographical variation (urban vs. suburban pricing patterns)
    • User demand patterns (real-time adjustment based on query volume)
  • Privacy-First Location Handling: To address location privacy concerns:
    • All processing happens on-device when possible
    • Location data is immediately anonymized and aggregated
    • Users can specify precision levels ("neighborhood", "city", "exact")
    • No persistent location history is stored without explicit consent

Evaluation and Results

I evaluated PricePoa against manual shopping and existing apps in a 4-week field study with 120 participants:

  • Baseline (Manual Shopping): Average basket cost: KSh 3,240 (±KSh 480)
  • Existing Price Comparison Apps: Average basket cost: KSh 2,980 (±KSh 420) - 8% savings
  • PricePoa: Average basket cost: KSh 2,760 (±KSh 380) - 15% savings vs. baseline

Beyond cost savings, PricePoa demonstrated significantly higher engagement:

  • Average weekly usage: 3.2 sessions per user (vs. 0.8 for traditional apps)
  • Retention rate after 4 weeks: 76% (vs. 22% for traditional apps)
  • User satisfaction score: 4.7/5 (vs. 3.2/5 for traditional apps)

The key insight was that reducing friction — by meeting users where they already were — drove adoption far more effectively than any feature advantage alone.

What I'd Do Differently

Building this taught me important lessons about deploying conversational agents in emerging markets:

  1. Start with Narrower Domain Focus: I initially tried to cover all grocery categories, but focusing on staples first (produce, dairy, grains) would have allowed deeper integration with specific store loyalty programs and more accurate seasonal pricing models.
  2. Design for Intermittent Connectivity: Many users experience fluctuating network quality. A queue-based system that processes queries when connectivity improves would have improved reliability compared to the real-time dependency approach.
  3. Incorporate Voice-First Interaction: Given varying literacy levels and the preference for voice notes in WhatsApp, implementing speech-to-text for query input and text-to-speech for results delivery would have increased accessibility.

Despite these limitations, PricePoa demonstrated that conversational agents can effectively deliver valuable services in markets where app fatigue is real and WhatsApp is the dominant platform. The system is currently processing an average of 850 weekly queries from active users in Nairobi, with ongoing partnerships forming with two supermarket chains to provide direct API access for even fresher pricing data.

The full implementation is open source on GitHub. I hope it inspires others to look beyond traditional app stores when building services for markets where messaging platforms are the de facto operating system — because sometimes the most powerful interface is the one people are already using.
